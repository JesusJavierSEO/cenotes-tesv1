Travel guide

The Best Cenotes
Near Merida

Distances, prices in USD and how to reach the most spectacular cenotes in Yucatan. Updated August 2026.

By Cenotes Homún·August 2026·7 min read

Merida is the best base in Mexico for exploring cenotes. Within a two-hour drive of the city you can reach some of the most striking swimming holes on the planet — from the turquoise water of the Homun cenote ring to the underground caverns near Valladolid. This guide covers which ones are worth your time, what they cost, and how to get to each.

1. Homun cenotes — the most authentic (45 min from Merida)

Homun is the most underrated cenote destination in Yucatan. Just 45 minutes southeast of Merida — roughly 30 miles — this small Maya town sits on more than 20 cenotes within the so-called cenote ring, the geological formation left by the Chicxulub meteorite 66 million years ago.

What sets Homun apart is the absence of mass tourism. While Ik Kil processes thousands of visitors a day, in Homun you can often swim alone or in a small group, with a local guide who grew up in the village.

Best cenotes in Homun

  • Cenote Santa Bárbara — The most complete. Four cenotes in one park (underground, open and semi-open). From $35 USD.
  • Cenote Santa Rosa — The most authentic and the cheapest. Emerald-green water unlike anything else in the area. From $19 USD.
  • Santa Cruz Cenotes — Colonial hacienda, turquoise underground cenote and a zip-line. From $36 USD.
⭐ Our pick

If you only have time for one stop in Homun, choose Santa Bárbara for the most complete experience, or Santa Rosa if you want something quieter and cheaper.

2. Cuzama cenotes — the most unusual ride (50 min from Merida)

Cuzama is famous for one reason: you reach the cenotes on a small cart pulled by a horse along the rails of an old henequen plantation. It's an experience that doesn't exist anywhere else. The three Cuzama cenotes — Chelentún, Chansinic'ché and Bolonchojol — are all underground, with dramatic shafts of light.

Cost: roughly $15–20 USD per person including the cart ride. Getting there: colectivo from Merida's South Terminal, 50 minutes, about $3 USD. Carts leave from the village; no reservation needed.

Worth knowing: Cuzama gets busy in high season, especially Easter week. The descent into the cenotes is by steep wooden ladders — not suitable for anyone with limited mobility.

3. Valladolid cenotes — the most photogenic (2 hrs from Merida)

Valladolid has two cenotes that appear in nearly every world ranking: Ik Kil and the Dzitnup pair (Xkekén and Samulá). The trade-off is distance — two hours each way from Merida — and crowds. Ik Kil in particular can mean a 30-minute wait in high season.

Cenote Ik Kil

The most famous cenote in Mexico and one of the most photographed on earth. A circular open cenote about 200 feet across with vines hanging all the way down to the water. Visually stunning, but busy. Entry is around $10 USD. It sits close to Chichén Itzá, so most people combine the two in one day.

Dzitnup cenotes (Xkekén and Samulá)

Two underground cenotes a few minutes apart, both with a single beam of light coming through the roof and hitting the water. Xkekén has the more theatrical stalactites; Samulá has tree roots dropping from the ceiling. Entry is about $8 USD for both.

Which one is right for you?

🏊 You want to swim in peace

Homun, on a morning visit. Arrive before 11 AM and you may have the water to yourself.

📸 You want the best photos

Ik Kil for the classic shot, Dzitnup for the light beam. Both mean an early start to beat the tour buses.

👨‍👩‍👧 You're traveling with kids

Santa Bárbara in Homun — shallow entry points, life jackets included and an elevator down to one of the cenotes.

🎭 You want something different

Cuzama, for the horse-drawn cart on the old plantation rails. Nothing else in Mexico works like it.
